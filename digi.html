Know your digital services, before you queue for them.

DigiLocker, Aadhaar and PAN sit behind most government paperwork citizens deal with. This register explains what each one actually does, lets you try safe practice tools, and lists welfare schemes worth knowing about.

Register I

Core digital identity services

Three services, three different jobs. Open an entry to read what it's for and try a practice tool that mimics its behaviour without touching any real record.

A personal, cloud-based document wallet. Instead of carrying physical certificates, a citizen keeps verified copies of their marksheets, licences and ID proofs in one place, ready to share when an office or form asks for them.

Practice locker — issue a mock document

A 12-digit identity number issued once per resident, used to prove who you are — not what you own or earn. The last digit is a checksum, meant to catch typing mistakes before a form is even submitted.

Practice checker — structural format only

A 10-character Permanent Account Number used to track financial activity — filing tax, opening a bank account, buying property above a threshold. Unlike Aadhaar, its structure encodes information: the fourth letter marks whether the holder is an individual, a firm, a trust and so on.
